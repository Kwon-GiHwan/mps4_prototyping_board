## Proposed Methods paragraph (verbatim, for insertion)

> **Use of AI and large language models.** Generative AI tools were used
> substantially in this work, and their role was confined to code, tooling and
> text rather than to the production of evidence. An LLM coding assistant was
> used to write and revise firmware diagnostic sources, host-side test and
> analysis scripts, build files, figure-generation code, and the automated
> consistency checkers used to audit this manuscript; 95 of the 335 commits in
> the project history are explicitly marked as generated with such a tool. The
> same class of tool was used to draft and revise portions of this manuscript's
> prose and to conduct internal review passes over it. All experimental values
> reported here were produced by deterministic execution rather than by a
> language model: by the Vela compiler, by Fast Models FVP processes, and by
> physical runs on an MPS4 board, each recorded through a byte-level provenance
> chain from model hash to linked executable hash. No commit that touched the
> frozen experimental evidence was generated by an AI tool. Every research
> decision — which configurations to measure, which analyses to preregister,
> which comparisons to refuse as unsupported, and whether to accept each stage
> of the work — was made by the author, recorded as an explicit decision before
> the corresponding work began, and is auditable in the project's frozen
> decision record.

**Length:** 5 sentences, ~180 words. Sits naturally at the end of the
Methodology section (Section 3) or as its own short subsection.

## Placement

The CFP requires the disclosure **in the Methods section**. This paper's Methods
is Section 3 (Methodology). Recommended placement: a final unnumbered paragraph
of Section 3, after Section 3.6, so it precedes the results that depend on the
tooling it describes.

**Not yet inserted into `main.tex`.** Insertion is a manuscript content addition
and is held pending the manager's answers to the two open points above, since
the first sentence's scope claim depends on them.

## What this disclosure deliberately does not say

- It does not claim "language editing only". The record contradicts that: 95
  marked commits produced source code and tooling, not prose polish.
- It does not imply an LLM produced experimental evidence. The path filter over
  those commits is empty for every frozen-evidence directory, and the values
  come from compiler, simulator and hardware execution.
- It does not attribute scientific judgement to a tool, and it does not claim
  the author reviewed every generated line — only that the decisions were the
  author's, which the frozen decision record supports.
- It gives a count (95 of 335) rather than a vague adverb, because the count is
  verifiable and an adverb is not.
